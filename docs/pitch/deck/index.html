01 / 06 · WhyLATAM Bank · Dispute agent

The customer's pain

You see a charge you never made. Then you typically wait 37 hours to hear back.

A customer who reports a charge they do not recognize waits a median of 37 hours for the bank's first response. One in ten waits 58 hours or more. Until then, the charge sits on their account with no answer.

37h

median wait for a first response to "Cargo no reconocido" (unrecognized charge)

Measured p90 58 h n = 7,567 of 12,297
Why disputes: a dispute can be checked in code against the customer's own ledger and decided by an explicit policy. Design argument

Source: docs/analysis/demand-report.md (finding 3: median 37.0 h, p90 58.0 h over the 7,567 of 12,297 complaints with a recorded first response). Measured on the challenge's synthetic LATAM Bank dataset, not a live bank.

02 / 06 · WhatThe product, on real app screens

A dispute agent that knows when to act, when to ask, and when to step aside.

1

Resolves, with proof

The record backs the claim, so a provisional credit lands in seconds, in the same chat.

2

Asks when it is unclear

Two matching charges: the customer picks from their own. It never guesses.

3

Protects, hands off

Likely fraud: the card is blocked first, then a person gets a complete file of verified facts.

03 / 06 · HowTrust by design

The model reads. The code decides.

The LLM only extracts and phrases

No refund comes from the model.

Policy and permissions live in code

No lookup can reach another customer's ledger.

Verified facts only

A persuasive claim the record contradicts still goes to a person.

Design argument, enforced in code: an exhaustive sweep over every reason and all 128 combinations of the other conditions shows the classifier never causes a credit (tests/test_policy_not_overridden.py) · 1,121 automated tests · docs/architecture-decisions.md dispute-agent AD-3, AD-5, AD-13 to AD-15.
